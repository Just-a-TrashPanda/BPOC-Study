# Chapter 8: Penal Code — Offenses & Classifications Question Bank

*This bank covers scenario-based application of Penal Code offenses cited in BPOC Chapter 8 (LOs 8.8–8.35). Each question presents a fact pattern and asks for two independent answers: which offense (if any) applies, and its punishment classification. Each half is scored independently (half credit each). Most offenses appear across 2-3 scenarios with different facts, enhancements, or defenses in play, mirroring how the same statute plays out differently depending on circumstances. A few scenarios are deliberately "no offense" fact patterns testing whether a defense, exception, or missing element defeats the case. Companion bank: `08-penal-code-definitions.md` (LOs 8.2-8.5, and the Definitions article of every chapter below).*

## LO 8.8 — Inchoate (Preparatory) Offenses: Attempt, Conspiracy, Solicitation (PC 15.01–15.03)

### Q1 — Scenario
**LO:** 8.8 · **Source:** PC 15.01, 30.02(c)(2)

A suspect pries open a first-floor window of an occupied home late at night, intending to climb in and steal jewelry. A motion-sensor light triggers and a dog begins barking inside; the suspect flees before ever entering the house. Officers recover pry-mark tools matching the window frame.

**Offense:**
- [ ] Burglary of a Habitation
- [x] Criminal Attempt (attempted burglary of a habitation)
- [ ] Criminal Trespass
- [ ] Criminal Mischief

**Classification:**
- [ ] Second Degree Felony
- [x] Third Degree Felony
- [ ] State Jail Felony
- [ ] Class A Misdemeanor

**Explanation:** Prying open the window is "more than mere preparation" toward burglary, satisfying Criminal Attempt (PC 15.01(a)) — but because the suspect never entered, completed Burglary of a Habitation didn't occur. PC 15.01(d) grades an attempt one category lower than the offense attempted. Burglary of a habitation is a felony of the second degree (PC 30.02(c)(2)), so the attempt is a third-degree felony.

---

### Q2 — Scenario
**LO:** 8.8 · **Source:** PC 15.01, 19.02(c)

Angry after a bar argument, a man fires a handgun at another patron as he flees the parking lot, intending to kill him. The shot misses entirely and the intended victim is uninjured. The shooter is arrested a block away, still armed.

**Offense:**
- [ ] Aggravated Assault
- [ ] Deadly Conduct
- [x] Criminal Attempt (attempted murder)
- [ ] Murder

**Classification:**
- [ ] First Degree Felony
- [x] Second Degree Felony
- [ ] Third Degree Felony
- [ ] State Jail Felony

**Explanation:** No death occurred, so Murder itself wasn't completed — but firing at the victim with intent to kill is more than mere preparation, satisfying attempt. Murder is a first-degree felony (PC 19.02(c)); an attempt is graded one category lower, making this a second-degree felony.

---

### Q3 — Scenario
**LO:** 8.8 · **Source:** PC 15.02, 29.03(b)

Two men agree to rob a check-cashing store at gunpoint. One buys ski masks and zip ties; the other steals a getaway car. Before they can carry out the plan, an informant tips off police and both are arrested with the masks, ties, and stolen car in hand.

**Offense:**
- [ ] Aggravated Robbery
- [x] Criminal Conspiracy (to commit aggravated robbery)
- [ ] Criminal Attempt (attempted aggravated robbery)
- [ ] Engaging in Organized Criminal Activity

**Classification:**
- [ ] First Degree Felony
- [x] Second Degree Felony
- [ ] Third Degree Felony
- [ ] State Jail Felony

**Explanation:** The agreement plus each man's overt act (buying masks/ties, stealing the getaway car) satisfies Criminal Conspiracy (PC 15.02) — no robbery ever occurred, so this isn't attempt or the completed offense. Aggravated Robbery is a first-degree felony (PC 29.03(b)); conspiracy is graded one category lower than the most serious object felony, making this a second-degree felony.

---

### Q4 — Scenario
**LO:** 8.8 · **Source:** PC 15.02, 31.03(e)(4)(A)

Two employees agree to strip and sell $40,000 worth of copper wiring from a construction site's electrical vault over a series of nights. One buys bolt cutters and a truck rental as the first overt act toward the plan. Security catches them scouting the site before any wire is taken.

**Offense:**
- [ ] Theft
- [x] Criminal Conspiracy (to commit theft)
- [ ] Burglary
- [ ] Criminal Mischief

**Classification:**
- [ ] Third Degree Felony
- [x] State Jail Felony
- [ ] Class A Misdemeanor
- [ ] Second Degree Felony

**Explanation:** No property was ever taken, so this isn't completed Theft — but the agreement plus the overt act (buying tools, renting a truck) is Criminal Conspiracy. A $40,000 theft would be a third-degree felony (PC 31.03(e)(5), $30,000–$150,000); conspiracy is graded one category lower, making this a state jail felony.

---

### Q5 — Scenario
**LO:** 8.8 · **Source:** PC 15.03(d)(1)

A man offers a coworker $10,000 to kill his business partner, describing exactly how and when he wants it done. The coworker secretly records the conversation and reports it to police before anything else happens.

**Offense:**
- [ ] Criminal Conspiracy
- [x] Criminal Solicitation
- [ ] Murder for Hire
- [ ] Criminal Attempt

**Classification:**
- [x] First Degree Felony
- [ ] Second Degree Felony
- [ ] Capital Felony
- [ ] Third Degree Felony

**Explanation:** Requesting that another person commit a capital offense — murder for remuneration is capital murder under PC 19.03(a)(3) — with intent that it be carried out is Criminal Solicitation (PC 15.03(a)). Because the solicited offense is a capital offense, PC 15.03(d)(1) makes the solicitation itself a first-degree felony (not a capital felony — no offense was actually committed).

### Q6 — Scenario
**LO:** 8.8 · **Source:** PC 15.03(d)(2)

A man asks an acquaintance to break into his ex-girlfriend's home and sexually assault her while threatening her with a knife, offering to pay him afterward. The acquaintance immediately reports the request to police, and officers corroborate it through the man's own text messages describing the plan.

**Offense:**
- [ ] Aggravated Sexual Assault
- [x] Criminal Solicitation
- [ ] Criminal Conspiracy
- [ ] Burglary

**Classification:**
- [ ] First Degree Felony
- [x] Second Degree Felony
- [ ] Third Degree Felony
- [ ] Capital Felony

**Explanation:** No assault occurred — the acquaintance never agreed to act, and PC 15.03(b) requires corroborating circumstances (satisfied here by the text messages), so this is Criminal Solicitation. Aggravated Sexual Assault is a first-degree felony; PC 15.03(d)(2) grades solicitation of a first-degree felony as a felony of the second degree.

## LO 8.9 — Criminal Homicide (PC 19.01–19.06)

### Q7 — Scenario
**LO:** 8.9 · **Source:** PC 19.02(b)(1), 19.02(c)

Officers are dispatched to a bar fight. The suspect accused the deceased of cheating at pool; a fight broke out, and the suspect struck the deceased in the head with a pool cue, killing him. Witnesses describe the blow as deliberate, not a wild swing.

**Offense:**
- [ ] Manslaughter
- [x] Murder
- [ ] Aggravated Assault
- [ ] Criminally Negligent Homicide

**Classification:**
- [ ] Second Degree Felony
- [x] First Degree Felony
- [ ] Capital Felony
- [ ] Third Degree Felony

**Explanation:** Intentionally striking someone in the head hard enough to kill them satisfies PC 19.02(b)(1) (intentionally or knowingly causing death) or (b)(2) (intent to cause serious bodily injury via an act clearly dangerous to life). Murder is a first-degree felony under PC 19.02(c).

---

### Q8 — Scenario
**LO:** 8.9 · **Source:** PC 19.02(a)(2), 19.02(d)

A man comes home early and finds his spouse with another person. In the immediate heat of the moment, before any chance to cool off, he strikes and kills the other person. At the punishment phase, the defense proves by a preponderance of the evidence that he acted under the immediate influence of sudden passion arising from adequate cause.

**Offense:**
- [x] Murder
- [ ] Manslaughter
- [ ] Criminally Negligent Homicide
- [ ] Aggravated Assault

**Classification:**
- [ ] First Degree Felony
- [x] Second Degree Felony
- [ ] Third Degree Felony
- [ ] State Jail Felony

**Explanation:** This is still Murder — sudden passion isn't a separate offense, it's a punishment-stage mitigator. PC 19.02(d) provides that if the defendant proves sudden passion arising from adequate cause by a preponderance of the evidence, the offense drops from a first-degree felony to a second-degree felony, rather than negating the murder charge itself.

---

### Q9 — Scenario
**LO:** 8.9 · **Source:** PC 19.02(b)(3), 19.02(c)

A man is cooking methamphetamine in his garage. The manufacturing process ignites, and the resulting explosion — an act clearly dangerous to human life inherent in the process — kills a neighbor standing next door. The man had no intent to hurt anyone but was in the midst of committing the felony of manufacturing a controlled substance.

**Offense:**
- [x] Murder
- [ ] Criminally Negligent Homicide
- [ ] Manslaughter
- [ ] Capital Murder

**Classification:**
- [x] First Degree Felony
- [ ] Second Degree Felony
- [ ] State Jail Felony
- [ ] Capital Felony

**Explanation:** PC 19.02(b)(3)'s felony-murder rule reaches a death caused by an act clearly dangerous to human life committed in the course of another felony (here, manufacturing a controlled substance) — intent to kill isn't required. Manufacturing isn't one of the felonies that elevates a killing to capital murder (kidnapping, burglary, robbery, aggravated sexual assault, arson, obstruction/retaliation, or certain terroristic threats), so this is Murder, a first-degree felony.

---

### Q10 — Scenario
**LO:** 8.9 · **Source:** PC 19.03(a)(2), 19.03(b)

During a theft at a convenience store, a suspect pulls a handgun and shoots and kills the clerk. The suspect is arrested a short time later.

**Offense:**
- [ ] Murder
- [x] Capital Murder
- [ ] Aggravated Robbery
- [ ] Manslaughter

**Classification:**
- [ ] First Degree Felony
- [x] Capital Felony
- [ ] Second Degree Felony
- [ ] Third Degree Felony

**Explanation:** Intentionally killing someone in the course of committing or attempting robbery is Capital Murder under PC 19.03(a)(2). PC 19.03(b) makes any offense under this section a capital felony.

---

### Q11 — Scenario
**LO:** 8.9 · **Source:** PC 19.03(a)(1), 19.03(b), 19.03(d)

An officer responds to a family disturbance call. While exiting the patrol vehicle in full uniform, the officer is ambushed and shot by the suspect. The officer dies from the injuries a week later. The suspect knew the victim was a police officer responding to the call.

**Offense:**
- [x] Capital Murder
- [ ] Murder
- [ ] Aggravated Assault on a Public Servant
- [ ] Manslaughter

**Classification:**
- [x] Capital Felony
- [ ] First Degree Felony
- [ ] Second Degree Felony
- [ ] Third Degree Felony

**Explanation:** Murdering a peace officer lawfully discharging an official duty, known by the actor to be an officer, is Capital Murder under PC 19.03(a)(1) — and PC 19.03(d) presumes the actor knew the victim was an officer if the officer was in a distinctive uniform. Capital murder is a capital felony under PC 19.03(b).

### Q12 — Scenario
**LO:** 8.9 · **Source:** PC 19.04(a), 19.04(b)

Officers respond to a single-vehicle crash. Witnesses report the driver was racing up and down a residential street at high speed before losing control, striking and killing an elderly pedestrian on the sidewalk. The driver had no intent to hurt anyone but was consciously aware of and disregarded the obvious, substantial risk created by racing through a residential area.

**Offense:**
- [ ] Criminally Negligent Homicide
- [x] Manslaughter
- [ ] Murder
- [ ] Intoxication Manslaughter

**Classification:**
- [x] Second Degree Felony
- [ ] First Degree Felony
- [ ] State Jail Felony
- [ ] Third Degree Felony

**Explanation:** Consciously disregarding a substantial and unjustifiable risk (racing through a residential street) that results in death is reckless conduct, satisfying Manslaughter (PC 19.04(a)). Manslaughter is a second-degree felony under PC 19.04(b).

---

### Q13 — Scenario
**LO:** 8.9 · **Source:** PC 19.04(a), 19.04(b), 28.09

A disgruntled plant employee recklessly bypasses a safety interlock on a chemical mixing line — conduct that also constitutes Tampering with a Consumer Product under PC 28.09 — without intending to hurt anyone. A coworker is exposed to the resulting toxic fumes and dies. At trial, the state proves the death was caused by the 28.09 tampering conduct.

**Offense:**
- [x] Manslaughter
- [ ] Criminally Negligent Homicide
- [ ] Murder
- [ ] Tampering with a Consumer Product (only)

**Classification:**
- [ ] Second Degree Felony
- [x] First Degree Felony
- [ ] Third Degree Felony
- [ ] Capital Felony

**Explanation:** Reckless conduct causing death is ordinarily Manslaughter graded as a second-degree felony — but PC 19.04(b) carves out an exception: if the state also proves the reckless conduct constituted an offense under Section 28.09 (Tampering with a Consumer Product) and that conduct caused the death, Manslaughter is elevated to a first-degree felony.

---

### Q14 — Scenario
**LO:** 8.9 · **Source:** PC 19.05(a), 19.05(b)

A gun owner leaves a loaded handgun on a nightstand and steps away to answer the door. Their five-year-old finds the gun, begins playing with it, and points it at a younger sibling. The gun discharges, killing the sibling. The owner is charged.

**Offense:**
- [x] Criminally Negligent Homicide
- [ ] Manslaughter
- [ ] Injury to a Child
- [ ] Murder

**Classification:**
- [x] State Jail Felony
- [ ] Second Degree Felony
- [ ] Third Degree Felony
- [ ] Class A Misdemeanor

**Explanation:** A reasonable gun owner ought to have been aware of the substantial risk in leaving a loaded firearm accessible to a five-year-old — failing to perceive that obvious risk is a gross deviation from the standard of care, satisfying criminal negligence rather than the higher "aware and disregarded" standard for recklessness. Criminally Negligent Homicide is a state jail felony under PC 19.05(b).

---

### Q15 — Scenario
**LO:** 8.9 · **Source:** PC 19.05(a), 19.05(b)

A subcontractor sets up scaffolding for a roofing crew, skipping several required safety pins because he's in a hurry to finish before a storm. He doesn't realize the scaffolding is unstable — a reasonable, competent scaffolder in his position would have caught the missing pins. A worker falls and dies when the scaffolding collapses.

**Offense:**
- [x] Criminally Negligent Homicide
- [ ] Manslaughter
- [ ] Injury to a Disabled Individual
- [ ] Deadly Conduct

**Classification:**
- [ ] Second Degree Felony
- [x] State Jail Felony
- [ ] Class A Misdemeanor
- [ ] Third Degree Felony

**Explanation:** The subcontractor wasn't aware the scaffolding was unstable (which would be recklessness) — he simply should have been, given his trade expertise, and his failure to catch it is a gross deviation from ordinary care. That's criminal negligence, not recklessness. Criminally Negligent Homicide is a state jail felony under PC 19.05(b).

---

### Q16 — Scenario
**LO:** 8.9 · **Source:** PC 9.32, 9.42

A homeowner wakes to find an intruder who has broken into the occupied home at 2 a.m. and is advancing toward the bedroom holding a crowbar. The homeowner, reasonably believing deadly force is immediately necessary to protect against the intruder's imminent use of unlawful force inside the home, shoots and kills the intruder. Investigation confirms forced entry, the crowbar, and no prior relationship between the two.

**Offense:**
- [ ] Murder
- [ ] Manslaughter
- [x] No offense — justified use of deadly force in defense of person/habitation
- [ ] Criminally Negligent Homicide

**Classification:**
- [ ] Second Degree Felony
- [ ] State Jail Felony
- [x] N/A — no offense committed
- [ ] Third Degree Felony

**Explanation:** Chapter 9's justification defenses (self-defense under PC 9.32 and the presumption of reasonableness for force against someone unlawfully and forcibly entering an occupied habitation under PC 9.42) mean a killing under these facts isn't a criminal homicide at all — it's justified. This is why officers investigate the totality of the circumstances (forced entry, weapon, relationship) before assuming a killing is criminal.

## LO 8.10 — Unlawful Restraint & Kidnapping (PC 20.02–20.04)

### Q17 — Scenario
**LO:** 8.10 · **Source:** PC 20.02(a), 20.02(c)

A man and woman argue in their apartment. When she tries to pick up the phone to call 911, he grabs her and physically prevents her from reaching it. She eventually breaks free, gets outside, and calls 911. Officers find no injuries and no weapon was involved.

**Offense:**
- [x] Unlawful Restraint
- [ ] Kidnapping
- [ ] Assault
- [ ] Aggravated Kidnapping

**Classification:**
- [x] Class A Misdemeanor
- [ ] State Jail Felony
- [ ] Third Degree Felony
- [ ] Second Degree Felony

**Explanation:** Intentionally restricting someone's movement without consent — even briefly and without secreting them anywhere — is Unlawful Restraint (PC 20.02(a)), not Kidnapping, because there was no abduction (no secreting/holding where not likely to be found, and no use or threat of deadly force). Baseline Unlawful Restraint is a Class A misdemeanor under PC 20.02(c).

---

### Q18 — Scenario
**LO:** 8.10 · **Source:** PC 20.02(a), 20.02(c)(1)

A babysitter, upset with the parents over unpaid wages, grabs their 6-year-old and holds the child in a locked bathroom for twenty minutes to "teach them a lesson," with no intent to take the child anywhere or harm them, before letting the child go. The parents are not relatives of the babysitter.

**Offense:**
- [x] Unlawful Restraint
- [ ] Kidnapping
- [ ] Endangering a Child
- [ ] Interference with Child Custody

**Classification:**
- [ ] Class A Misdemeanor
- [x] State Jail Felony
- [ ] Third Degree Felony
- [ ] Felony of the Second Degree

**Explanation:** Restraining the child in the bathroom without consent is Unlawful Restraint. Because the person restrained was a child younger than 17, PC 20.02(c)(1) elevates the offense from a Class A misdemeanor to a state jail felony.

---

### Q19 — Scenario
**LO:** 8.10 · **Source:** PC 20.03(a), 20.03(c)

During a custody dispute, an ex-spouse takes the couple's child in defiance of a standing court order and flees with the child, refusing to disclose their location. A spot broadcast leads officers to locate and arrest the ex-spouse and recover the child unharmed.

**Offense:**
- [ ] Interference with Child Custody
- [x] Kidnapping
- [ ] Unlawful Restraint
- [ ] Aggravated Kidnapping

**Classification:**
- [ ] State Jail Felony
- [x] Third Degree Felony
- [ ] Second Degree Felony
- [ ] First Degree Felony

**Explanation:** Secreting the child where the other parent isn't likely to find them satisfies "abduct," making this Kidnapping (PC 20.03(a)) rather than mere restraint — and it's charged under this chapter rather than (or in addition to) Interference with Child Custody. Kidnapping is a third-degree felony under PC 20.03(c).

---

### Q20 — Scenario
**LO:** 8.10 · **Source:** PC 20.03(a), 20.03(b), 20.03(c)

A woman's uncle, worried she's making poor decisions, takes her 10-year-old niece for a weekend without telling the parents where they went, intending only to look after the child until things "calm down" — with no intent to use force and no plan to keep the child indefinitely. He is not the child's legal guardian.

**Offense:**
- [x] Kidnapping
- [ ] Unlawful Restraint
- [ ] Interference with Child Custody
- [ ] No offense — relative affirmative defense applies

**Classification:**
- [x] Third Degree Felony
- [ ] Class A Misdemeanor
- [ ] State Jail Felony
- [ ] N/A — no offense committed

**Explanation:** Secreting the child from the parents technically satisfies abduction, so this is still Kidnapping as charged — but the uncle can raise PC 20.03(b)'s affirmative defense at trial (relative, no intent to use/threaten deadly force, sole intent to assume lawful control). An affirmative defense is something the defendant must prove at trial; it doesn't change how the offense is charged or classified going in, which is why officers still investigate and charge it as Kidnapping, a third-degree felony under PC 20.03(c).

---

### Q21 — Scenario
**LO:** 8.10 · **Source:** PC 20.04(a)(4), 20.04(c)

Officers respond to a robbery in progress at a convenience store. As an officer arrives, the suspect runs back inside, grabs the clerk in a chokehold, and holds a knife to the clerk's throat, threatening to kill the clerk if the officer doesn't let him go.

**Offense:**
- [ ] Kidnapping
- [x] Aggravated Kidnapping
- [ ] Aggravated Robbery
- [ ] Unlawful Restraint

**Classification:**
- [ ] Third Degree Felony
- [ ] Second Degree Felony
- [x] First Degree Felony
- [ ] Capital Felony

**Explanation:** Abducting the clerk (holding them with a knife, using them as a shield/hostage to negotiate escape) satisfies Aggravated Kidnapping under PC 20.04(a)(2). Aggravated Kidnapping is a first-degree felony under PC 20.04(c).

---

### Q22 — Scenario
**LO:** 8.10 · **Source:** PC 20.04(b), 20.04(c), 20.04(d)

A suspect abducts a former coworker at gunpoint, driving him around for two hours while threatening him, then releases him unharmed at a gas station of his own accord before any ransom demand or further harm occurs. At the punishment phase, the defense proves the voluntary release in a safe place by a preponderance of the evidence.

**Offense:**
- [x] Aggravated Kidnapping
- [ ] Kidnapping
- [ ] Unlawful Restraint
- [ ] Deadly Conduct

**Classification:**
- [ ] First Degree Felony
- [x] Second Degree Felony
- [ ] Third Degree Felony
- [ ] Class A Misdemeanor

**Explanation:** Using or exhibiting a deadly weapon during an abduction is Aggravated Kidnapping under PC 20.04(b), regardless of whether any of the listed specific intents (ransom, hostage, etc.) is proven. It's still charged as Aggravated Kidnapping — but PC 20.04(d) allows the defendant to reduce the punishment range from a first-degree to a second-degree felony by proving voluntary release in a safe place.

## LO 8.11 — Smuggling & Trafficking of Persons (PC 20.05, 20A.02–20A.03)

### Q23 — Scenario
**LO:** 8.11 · **Source:** PC 20.05(a)(1), 20.05(b)

During a traffic stop for a defective taillight, an officer hears screaming and knocking from the trunk. Inside, the officer finds a woman and her two children. The woman says she paid the driver $1,000 to smuggle her and her children past a Border Patrol checkpoint; officers recover $1,000 in cash under the driver's seat during an inventory search.

**Offense:**
- [x] Smuggling of Persons
- [ ] Trafficking of Persons
- [ ] Unlawful Restraint
- [ ] Kidnapping

**Classification:**
- [ ] Second Degree Felony
- [x] Third Degree Felony
- [ ] First Degree Felony
- [ ] State Jail Felony

**Explanation:** Using a vehicle to transport and conceal individuals from a peace officer to help them evade lawful immigration enforcement is Smuggling of Persons (PC 20.05(a)(1)). Baseline smuggling is a third-degree felony under PC 20.05(b) (with a 10-year term), before any of the listed aggravators (serious bodily injury risk, child victim, pecuniary benefit, firearm, or the transporter fleeing from officers) are shown.

---

### Q24 — Scenario
**LO:** 8.11 · **Source:** PC 20.05(a)(1), 20.05(b)(1)(B)

Officers stop a van after a pursuit. Inside, they find six people concealed in a hidden compartment, including a 15-year-old traveling alone. The driver admits he was paid to transport the group and knew a minor was among them.

**Offense:**
- [x] Smuggling of Persons
- [ ] Trafficking of Persons
- [ ] Continuous Trafficking of Persons
- [ ] Endangering a Child

**Classification:**
- [ ] Third Degree Felony
- [x] Second Degree Felony
- [ ] First Degree Felony
- [ ] State Jail Felony

**Explanation:** This is still Smuggling of Persons (transporting to conceal individuals from law enforcement), not Trafficking — nothing here shows the driver intended forced labor, services, or sexual exploitation, which is what separates trafficking from smuggling. But PC 20.05(b)(1)(B) elevates smuggling from a third-degree to a second-degree felony when the smuggled individual is a child younger than 18.

---

### Q25 — Scenario
**LO:** 8.11 · **Source:** PC 20A.02(a)(3), 20A.02(b)

Following up on a smuggling case, an adult victim tells officers she was compelled through threats and coercion into prostitution and was being delivered to a brothel to work off the remainder of the smuggling debt she owed the suspect.

**Offense:**
- [ ] Smuggling of Persons
- [x] Trafficking of Persons
- [ ] Promotion of Prostitution
- [ ] Compelling Prostitution

**Classification:**
- [x] Second Degree Felony
- [ ] Third Degree Felony
- [ ] First Degree Felony
- [ ] State Jail Felony

**Explanation:** Once the facts show the suspect used coercion to force the victim into prostitution (as opposed to merely transporting her to evade law enforcement), the offense becomes Trafficking of Persons under PC 20A.02(a)(3). Baseline trafficking of an adult is a second-degree felony under PC 20A.02(b) (first-degree if the victim is a child/disabled individual or suffers serious bodily injury or death).

---

### Q26 — Scenario
**LO:** 8.11 · **Source:** PC 20A.02(a)(5), 20A.02(b)(1)

Investigators determine that over several months, a suspect recruited and transported a 15-year-old runaway, intending for the child to perform forced labor at a restaurant the suspect controlled, regardless of whether the suspect actually knew or asked the runaway's age.

**Offense:**
- [x] Trafficking of Persons
- [ ] Smuggling of Persons
- [ ] Continuous Trafficking of Persons
- [ ] Enticing a Child

**Classification:**
- [ ] Second Degree Felony
- [x] First Degree Felony
- [ ] Third Degree Felony
- [ ] Capital Felony

**Explanation:** Trafficking a child with intent that the child engage in forced labor satisfies PC 20A.02(a)(5) regardless of whether the actor knew the victim's age. Because the victim is a child, PC 20A.02(b)(1) makes this a first-degree felony rather than the baseline second degree.

---

### Q27 — Scenario
**LO:** 8.11 · **Source:** PC 20A.03(a), 20A.03(e)

Over a four-month investigation, officers document that a suspect trafficked the same runaway teenager into forced prostitution on at least six separate, well-documented occasions spanning more than 30 days.

**Offense:**
- [ ] Trafficking of Persons
- [x] Continuous Trafficking of Persons
- [ ] Promotion of Prostitution
- [ ] Compelling Prostitution

**Classification:**
- [ ] Second Degree Felony
- [x] First Degree Felony, with an enhanced 25-year minimum term
- [ ] First Degree Felony, with the standard 5-year minimum term
- [ ] Capital Felony

**Explanation:** Two or more trafficking violations against the same victim spanning 30 or more days is Continuous Trafficking of Persons (PC 20A.03(a)) rather than separate counts of ordinary trafficking. PC 20A.03(e) makes this a first-degree felony with an elevated minimum — life or a term of not more than 99 years or less than 25 years — harsher than an ordinary first-degree felony's usual 5-year floor.

## LO 8.12 — Sexual Offenses (PC 21.02–21.16)

### Q28 — Scenario
**LO:** 8.12 · **Source:** PC 21.02(b), 21.02(h)

A school counselor learns that a 9-year-old student's 30-year-old uncle has been sexually abusing her by forcing oral contact, and that this has happened repeatedly over the past three months.

**Offense:**
- [x] Continuous Sexual Abuse of Young Child or Disabled Individual
- [ ] Indecency with a Child
- [ ] Sexual Assault
- [ ] Aggravated Sexual Assault

**Classification:**
- [x] First Degree Felony
- [ ] Second Degree Felony
- [ ] Third Degree Felony
- [ ] Capital Felony

**Explanation:** Two or more acts of sexual abuse against a child younger than 14, committed by someone 17 or older, over a period of 30 or more days, is Continuous Sexual Abuse rather than separate counts of the underlying abuse. It's a first-degree felony under PC 21.02(h).

Over a two-month span, a stepfather repeatedly engages in sexual contact with his adult stepdaughter, who has an intellectual disability that leaves her unable to appraise the nature of the conduct or resist it. Investigators document at least three separate incidents.

**Offense:**
- [x] Continuous Sexual Abuse of Young Child or Disabled Individual
- [ ] Sexual Assault
- [ ] Indecency with a Child
- [ ] Injury to a Disabled Individual

**Classification:**
